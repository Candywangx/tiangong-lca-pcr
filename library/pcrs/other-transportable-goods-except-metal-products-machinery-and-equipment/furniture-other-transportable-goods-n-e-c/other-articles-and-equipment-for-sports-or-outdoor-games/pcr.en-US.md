# Hand-stitched PU-skin inflatable association-football manufacture



## 1. Scope and Applicability


This PCR covers new complete hand-stitched inflatable association footballs made from whole supplied PU-faced textile-backed laminate and complete formed natural-latex bladder/valve. Include receipt, panel cutting, actual printing/drying, bladder preparation/leak testing, assembly/hand stitching/closure, inflation/rounding and complete-ball first quality release. Packaging-only supply or packaging purchased finished balls is not this manufacture. Manufacturer anatomy, real operations and supply-form documents are a transparent technical synthesis, not a claim of one SKU, factory or order. Declare actual backing fibres/layer build/cushion formulation and supplier inclusions per configuration; no default layer count, glue recipe, stitch count, pressure, temperature/time, ball mass, lifetime, certification or yield.

Exclude thermal/ultrasonic bonding, machine stitching and hybrid bonded seams; solid/foamed rubber balls, other ball sports/toys, bladder/valve/sheet/resin/needle/pump-only supplies and training services. Footwear, rubber/laminate sheet production and sewing-machine manufacture cannot substitute for complete-ball methodology. Actual packing, storage, handling, dispatch and postrelease work retain a separate original-cohort service account, including subcontract locations.

| rule_id | Applies to | Formula or rule | Inputs | Output | source_ids |

| single_quotient | all inventory rows | x = A / Q. This is the sole normalization, after original raw unit conversion and actual burden attribution. For manufacturing A is the attributable converted raw load plus unassigned inherited opening raw/WIP/non-WIP vectors minus only still-unassigned closing vectors and any once-justified true other-function/cohort handover; already-net consumption receives no second stock correction and released closing FG is not subtracted. Q is actual unique first-release kg for the corresponding cohort; Q=0 retains load without x.
